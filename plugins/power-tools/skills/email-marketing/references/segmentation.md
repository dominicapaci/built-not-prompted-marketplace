# Segmenting your list

Sending every email to everyone is the fastest way to train people to ignore you. Segmenting means the right people get the right email.

## One list, then segments

Keep one master list. Do not split people into many separate lists, that just creates duplicates and messy data. Instead use segments: dynamic groups that update themselves based on rules.

## The segments worth having

For most creators, four cover it:

- New: joined in the last 30 days.
- Engaged: opened or clicked in the last 60 days.
- Lapsed: no activity in 90-plus days.
- Customers versus non-customers.

## Engagement-based sending

This is the single highest-impact move on this page. Instead of sending every email to the whole list, send by how engaged someone is:

- Recently active: send everything.
- Cooling off: send less often, your best content only.
- Quiet for 90-plus days: move them into a win-back sequence, not the regular flow.

The result is better open and click rates, far fewer spam complaints, and steady revenue, because you stopped mailing people who were dragging your reputation down.

## RFM, the simple version

For creators who sell, group buyers by recency, frequency, and spend. The quick version is recency alone: bought in the last 30 days, 31 to 90, 91 to 180, 180-plus. Each group gets different messaging.

## Do not over-segment

A segment needs enough people in it to be worth the effort, usually a few hundred at least. Interest and behavior are worth segmenting on. Name and demographics alone are not. Review your segments a few times a year and retire the ones you never send to.
