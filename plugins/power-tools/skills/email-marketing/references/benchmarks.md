# Email benchmarks

Numbers to measure against. Find your worst gap versus these and fix that first.

## Core metrics

| Metric | Good | Strong | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate | 2 to 3% | 4%-plus | Below 1% |
| Click-to-open rate | 10 to 15% | 20%-plus | Below 5% |
| Unsubscribe rate | Under 0.2% | Under 0.1% | Above 0.5% |
| Bounce rate | Under 2% | Under 1% | Above 3% |
| Spam complaint rate | Under 0.1% | Under 0.05% | Above 0.3% |
| List growth | 3 to 5% a month | 5%-plus | Negative |

Open rate is left off on purpose. Since Apple Mail privacy changes it is unreliable. Use click-based metrics as your primary read.

## By email type

| Type | Typical click rate |
|---|---|
| Welcome | 5 to 8% |
| Cart abandonment | 5 to 10% |
| Transactional | 5 to 15% |
| Promotional | 2 to 3% |
| Newsletter | 3 to 5% |
| Win-back | 1 to 2% |

Welcome, cart, and transactional emails get read far more than promotional ones. That is where to put your best effort.

## Sending frequency

| Type of sender | Reasonable range |
|---|---|
| Newsletter or creator | 1 to 3 times a week |
| Ecommerce | 3 to 5 times a week for engaged subscribers |
| Lower-engagement subscribers | About once a week |

Track revenue or clicks per email sent, not just totals. When an extra send stops adding anything, you have found your ceiling.

## Why email is worth doing properly

Email returns roughly $36 for every $1 spent, higher than any other channel. It is also the only audience you own outright. No algorithm sits between you and the inbox.
