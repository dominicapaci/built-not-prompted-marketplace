# Email sequences

Automated sequences out-earn one-off campaigns by a wide margin, and they run without you once built. Set these up before you worry about regular campaigns.

## Welcome sequence

The first emails a new subscriber gets. The highest open rates you will ever see, often 50% or more. Build this first.

5 to 6 emails over about two weeks:

- Day 0, immediate: deliver what they signed up for. Set expectations for what is coming. Ask one question and invite a reply.
- Day 2: your story. Who you are and why you do this.
- Day 4: social proof. A result, a case study, or a short testimonial.
- Day 7: your best content or product, pointed at what they told you on Day 0.
- Day 10: a soft offer.
- Day 14: set the ongoing rhythm and link a preferences page.

## Nurture sequence

For subscribers who are interested but not ready. Move them from aware to ready over several weeks.

- Weeks 1 to 2, problem: name the problem clearly, show you understand it, teach something.
- Weeks 3 to 4, solution: show the ways to solve it, including yours, honestly.
- Weeks 5 to 6, product: how your offer solves it, with proof, then a clear offer.

## Cart abandonment

For creators selling a product. Around 70% of carts get abandoned. Three emails:

- 1 to 4 hours: a simple reminder. No discount.
- 24 hours: handle the objection. Reviews, guarantee, shipping, FAQ.
- 48 hours: a small incentive, only if the margin allows, and only for first-time abandoners.

## Win-back

For subscribers who have gone quiet, around 60 to 90 days inactive. 3 to 5 emails:

1. "We miss you." Remind them of the value.
2. What is new since they last looked.
3. A direct question, plus a link to update their preferences.
4. The breakup email. "Should we say goodbye?" This one gets the highest reply rate of the set.
5. Goodbye, with a one-click resubscribe. Then suppress the non-responders.

A quiet list is not a free list. Unengaged subscribers drag down deliverability for everyone, so let them go.

## Post-purchase

After someone buys. Turns a buyer into a repeat buyer:

- Immediately: thank you, and what happens next.
- Day 2 to 3: getting started, how to use it well.
- Day 7 to 10: a check-in and an offer to help.
- Day 14: ask for a review.
- Day 21 to 30: a referral ask, or a related product.

## How automation triggers work

A sequence runs on triggers and conditions inside your email platform.

- Entry triggers start the sequence: a signup, a tag added, a purchase, a link click, entering a segment.
- Conditions sit between emails: if-then branches and wait steps.
- Exit conditions stop it: the goal is met, they unsubscribe, or a time limit is hit.

Trigger types: time-based (a set number of days after an event), behavior-based (they did something), and event-based (a purchase, a registration, a trial start).
