# Email marketing

Two jobs in one skill: writing the email, and improving the system around it. Use it to draft a newsletter, build a full sequence, or audit an email setup that is underperforming.

Email copy should sound like a person wrote it, not a brand. Write to one reader. If the humanizer tool is installed, run finished drafts through it.

## Step 1: figure out which job this is

- Writing job: they want an email, a newsletter issue, or a sequence drafted.
- Strategy job: they want better open or click rates, better deliverability, or a smarter list setup.

Most requests are the writing job. Handle that, and pull in the strategy references when the numbers are the real problem.

## Writing an email

Get three things before drafting: who it is going to, the single action you want them to take, and the sender's voice. One email, one call to action.

### Anatomy

```
From:    [Name] from [Brand]
Subject: [hook plus benefit], around 50 characters
Preview: [extends the subject, does not repeat it], 90 to 100 characters

[Greeting, to one person]
[Hook: name the reader's pain or desire in the first line]
[Value: the main content, short paragraphs, scannable]
[Proof: a result, a number, or a quick testimonial, optional]
[One CTA, as a button if the platform allows]
[P.S.: restate the hook or add a reason to act now]
[Signature with a human name]
```

### Subject lines

64% of the open decision is the subject line. Keep it short, around 50 characters or less. Pick a formula by goal:

| Formula | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Question | "Still stuck on [pain]?" | Engagement |
| How-to | "How to [outcome] in [time]" | Education |
| Curiosity | "The [thing] most [audience] miss" | Opens |
| Social proof | "How [name] got [result]" | Conversion |
| Urgency | "[X] hours left: [offer]" | Promotions |
| Personal | "[First name], quick question" | Replies |

### Frameworks

Pick one by the email's goal:

- PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution). The reliable default for most emails.
- AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action). Promotional emails.
- BAB (Before, After, Bridge). Transformation stories and case studies.
- 1-3-1 newsletter. One main story, three short items, one CTA.

### Body rules

Key message first, inverted pyramid. Short paragraphs, built for the 60%-plus who read on a phone. Write the draft, then cut about 30%. Keep roughly three value emails for every one that sells.

## Building a sequence

For any multi-email flow, read `references/sequences.md`. It has day-by-day blueprints for the welcome, nurture, cart abandonment, win-back, and post-purchase sequences, plus how automation triggers work.

Start with the welcome sequence. It earns the highest open rates a sender ever gets, and it runs on autopilot once built.

## Fixing the strategy

When the problem is the numbers, not the words:

- Compare their metrics against `references/benchmarks.md`. Find the worst gap and fix that one first.
- Inbox placement and deliverability: `references/deliverability.md`.
- List setup and segmenting: `references/segmentation.md`.

One rule before reading anything: open rates have been unreliable since Apple Mail started pre-loading images. Treat click rate as the real signal.

## Anti-patterns

| Do not | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Buy a list | Wrecks deliverability | Build it from real opt-ins |
| Send everyone everything | Irrelevance drives unsubscribes | Segment by engagement |
| Stack multiple CTAs | Splits attention, fewer clicks | One CTA per email |
| Hide the unsubscribe | Illegal, and triggers spam complaints | Make it one click |
| Batch and blast | No relevance, no timing | Trigger emails off behavior |
